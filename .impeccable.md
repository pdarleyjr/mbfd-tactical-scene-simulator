## Design Context

### Users

One operator: an MBFD instructor or company officer running a tabletop evolution for firefighters and officers, at a desk, on a tablet, or in front of a classroom display. They need to put apparatus on the scene in seconds, lay and adjust hose, see water supply at a glance, run the clock and injects, and present without fighting the software. Working alone is the default. Working together is deliberate: an instructor starts a shared simulation and gives people an invite link; there are still no accounts or PINs.

### Brand Personality

Operational, composed, and exact. It should feel like dependable emergency-services equipment: legible under pressure, restrained enough not to compete with the incident scene, confident without command-center theatrics.

### Aesthetic Direction

Dark, compact chrome around the vivid aerial scene. Colors: asphalt charcoal surfaces, turnout-canvas tan for structure and secondary numbers, apparatus red only for live or destructive state (Present, running clock, injects, delete), water-supply blue for action, selection, and progress, green for supplied/ok, amber for warnings and anything uncalibrated. Hose colors: 5″ supply yellow, 3″ white, 1¾″ attack red-orange, always with a dark outline over imagery. Depth is borders and quiet surface shifts. Barlow for titles, labels, and numbers (tabular); Source Sans 3 for body text.

Signature: the map fills the screen; tools sit in a slim left dock with a contextual tray; the selection opens one inspector on the right; drawing shows one prompt bar at the bottom. Unit labels and hose lengths keep a constant screen size at every zoom.

### Design Principles

1. The map is the home screen once a simulation is open. The only screen before it is a plain two-choice start screen (your own simulation, or a shared one); nobody is ever dropped into someone else's scene by visiting the address.
2. Every primary action works by touch, mouse, and keyboard; touch targets are at least 42px and drag always has a tap alternative.
3. Never show feet without a calibration. Never present a configured default as a verified MBFD fact.
4. One home for each capability: placing is in the dock/tray, editing is in the inspector, training is in the Training panel, scene definition is in Scenario settings.
5. Progressive disclosure: advanced options live in the inspector and settings, not on the map.
6. Status is always visible: save state and device scope, calibration, water supply, clock.
7. Avoid card grids, glow, glass, fake CAD maps, generic apparatus rectangles, giant panels, em-dashes in UI copy, and desktop-only drag-and-drop.
