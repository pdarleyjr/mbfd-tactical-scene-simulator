## Design Context

### Users

Firefighters, company officers, incident-command trainees, and instructors use this application during classroom evolutions. Participants need to join in seconds, understand the live fireground at a glance, place their unit, and deploy semantically correct hose/evolutions with touch, mouse, or keyboard. Instructors need a denser control surface for scenario setup, injects, permissions, presentation, and comparison without crowding participant screens.

### Brand Personality

Operational, composed, and exact. The interface should feel like dependable emergency-services equipment: immediately legible under pressure, restrained enough not to compete with the incident scene, and confident without theatrical command-center effects.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use understated dark incident-command chrome around the vivid aerial scene. The color world comes from asphalt charcoal, turnout-canvas tan, apparatus red, hydrant yellow, command-board white, hose yellow, and water-supply blue. Blue communicates ordinary action and progress; saturated red is reserved for live, destructive, or emergency state. Depth is borders and quiet surface shifts, not glow, glass, or dramatic shadows. Typography should be compact and highly legible, with tabular numerals for timers and measurements.

The product signature is a map-dominant incident rail: a compact roster/evolution strip that exposes unit ownership, connection availability, and collaboration state without turning the experience into a generic dashboard.

### Design Principles

1. Keep the tactical map at approximately 85-90% of usable participant space whenever the viewport permits.
2. Every primary action works by touch and pointer, with a keyboard alternative; touch targets are at least 48px.
3. Preserve one logical world coordinate system across operations, 300 planning, presentation, and every viewport.
4. Use color semantically and never as the only distinction between Operations and 300 Plan.
5. Prefer progressive disclosure: participant tools stay compact while instructor, builder, and review controls live in purpose-built routes.
6. Use a 4px spacing base, restrained corner radii, subtle border depth, and explicit loading, empty, offline, error, focus, and disabled states.
7. Avoid card grids, neon/glow styling, fake CAD maps, generic apparatus rectangles, giant panels, and desktop-only drag-and-drop.
